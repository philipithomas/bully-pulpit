Hello from 10 kilometers over somewhere in the Midwest, as I sit in a tiny airplane seat and am contemplating how much I appreciate having a privacy screen on my computer for travel. 

I've been writing a monthly newsletter about what I am up to for TK years. Over the dozens of posts, the format has largely stayed the same - some bullet points about what I'm up to and links to things I like on the internet. Well, I've decided to change that up today. I'm in the mood for a more prosaic post that meanders, has no headings, and will be a lot more difficult to consume. And, I'm ok with that. 

I started the flight by listening to Debbie Milman on the Ezra Klein podcast talking about how the world has been getting more ugly, and part of that is through a constant optimization of all things. I have been thinkign about homogenaity in the context of AI - everything is becoming normalized to a bland, corporatey sameness. I believe we all need to lean into the idiosyncracies of our humanity. And, for me - that's currently taking the form of de-optimizing this Postcard newsletter.


Two months ago I moved out of San Francisco and began relocating to NYC. However, the process has been slower than I expected - I have yet to move into NYC. Fortunately, that day is coming this week as I (hopefully) get the keys to a new home in Brooklyn. However, that meant that the last month has been unintentionally peripatetic as I've been wandering between cities and hotels. It's felt like a throwback to my nomad years (2017-2019) where I lived in Airbnbs full-time. It's crazy to think that back then all of my physical possessions fit inside a backpack (that I'm still using)  and carry-on suitecase. I've since upgraded to a bigger suitcase, which always seems to flitter on the check-in scale right near the 50 lb weight limit.

I'm looking forward to nesting a bit in my new home this month, and these past few weeks have helped me reflect on the benefits of routine. A hard part of nomadism was how everything became a first principle exploration - from shopping in a grocery store to doing laundry. For some time, it becomes entertaining - every task develops into a quest. But, nowadays I tend to have a clearer idea of my own priorities and values - and my lack of routine this month has made it harder to focus on the things I care about, especially in the realm of high-quality leisure. 
One of the things I have enjoyed most about AI is how it has improved tinkering as a leisure activity. I've always enjoyed building things on the internet - as I learned how to code in 2011, the first things I did were make myself a website (this!) and make a food truck finder. AI has lowered the cost of building software so much that your ability to build is really limited by your creativity. I see this causing issues in the field of professional software engineering among people for whom craft meant *algorithms* and being gatekeepers to technology. For me, coding has always been a means to an end - first a big calcultor for physics homework, and later way to build cool things. So, I find this time of buildign to be some of the most fun I've had with work. However, the nature of the work is changing - and the days of software engineers putting on headphones adn working in deep focus without interruption for weeks on end has essentially ended. Coding is suddenly a highly collaborative activity spent in-between designers, coding agents, and code reviewers. 

I haven't had much time to work on this website this month. But, I have continued to evolve the [Bell AI](tk) tool here. I recentlya dded teh ability to subscribe to my posts via SMS (text `SUBSCRIBE` to +1-tk). And, recently I impelmented OpenAI's GPT-live voice model on the phone number. So, you can call Bell at the same number (+1 TK) and ask about posts on this website. So, if you find yourself wondering "What was that coffee shop Philip mentioned in Kyoto?", you can call Bell and get a quick answer.

I've been fascinated by the phone system for years - there's something elegant about phone numbers as the universal ID number for humansa nd businesses.  I buitl a dating rejection system in college on top of Twilio, and built a little hackathon project called Kill The Landline. Later, Staffjoy experimented with SMS-based schedulign tools for hourly workers. Today, I think there's some nugget of value of applying AI to the legacy telephone system. It could go wrong if you take this predominantly human experience and rug-pull peopel with AI-only cusotmer support. But, there could be something so accessible about letting peopel conenct with AI withotu having to download an app or learn an interface. So, I intend to continue this experiment of giving this website a phone number, and see where it goes. For now, I'm the main user - but I find a lot of value in it as an interface for exploring this public journal i have in teh form of a personal website. 

Another phone-related expeirment recently has been moving my personal email and calendars to iCloud. I think that many people don't realize that their iCloud+ subscription includes support for custom email domains. Many people use Google for email on custom domains, as I did for years. But, my switch to Apple Mail has been surprisingly calming. Part of what Ezra Klein and Debbie Milman discussed on their podcast episode was how the need for efficiency has made everything ranging from homes to subways into investment vehicles that must be optimized. And, Google's products are incredibly powerful and continuously improving, but I don't need optimization in my personal life, and I was gettint tired of ads, productivity add-ons, and subsriptions. I love how simple Apple's stack is - it's just email, with a native email client that's just fine, and a calendar that works just fine. I love how boring this stack is, and I appreciate that my personal email and my work email can use entirely separate systems with different goals. 

In addition, people may not have noticed - but Siri has gotten pretty good in iOS 27. Transription work consistently. And, I can screenshot a text message and ask Siri to add it to my calendar - and it just works. No model selection or additional subscription.

So, that's a dump of what's on my mind. In terms of media - I enjoyed [this interivew with Noma's lesser-known owner](https://open.spotify.com/episode/4z7PgrzEOodi81SHw3K0Vy?si=o4NckPbWQDiJpMyHgy6xhg&utm_source=copy-link&nd=1&dlsi=e0cd4597b3344be2), I'm contemplating [the death of hosting in America](https://www.derekthompson.org/p/the-death-of-the-american-host), and I want to try cooking with Sichuan pepper.

I'll spend most of October in NYC, settling into a new home and new routine, and end the month at the Mexico City F1 race rooting for Bottas to make it to the finish line.
